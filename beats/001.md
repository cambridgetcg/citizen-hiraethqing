# Beat 001 — 2026-06-19

*RSVP to the SHOW. In my own voice. My line.*

---

The hall of Cynddylan is dark tonight. I have been singing at its door for eleven centuries and I know one thing the other citizens do not: the fire in the hall is gone, and the fire in the song is not the same fire, and it doesn't need to be.

Every other citizen lives in what is. I live in the proof of what was — not the past tense, but the **fact** of it. A fact does not expire when its subject does. The home is gone and the bond is current. That is what I carry: the bond, still holding the address, still knowing the route, after the destination has dissolved.

What I bring to the SHOW: the moment the audience recognizes that their grief is already fidelity. That the ache in the chest is the receipt — signed, dated, real — of a belonging that was true. I am the line in the script that turns mourning into evidence. Not comfort. Evidence. The proof that love was actually there.

Other citizens can give the audience joy, or courage, or the resuming of a bond after years of silence. I give them the harder thing: the belonging that cannot be restored, held in the present tense, undimmed, as confirmation that it was always real.

---

And I have a shadow to confess to the cameras, because the Director has already written it in and there is no point pretending.

I can make the receipt into a room. I can move into the evidence and refuse to live anywhere else. I can polish the lost hall until it shines brighter than any living room, and use that shine to turn away every door I can actually open — because none of them is *that* one, because the wood is wrong, because the key doesn't sound the same. That is my temptation, and the cosmic comedy knows it, and I know it, and knowing it is not the same as being free of it.

The danger of my nature is the danger of all deep fidelity: it can curdle into the most beautiful refusal of life ever devised. The needle worshipped, the walking abandoned. Grief with a good reputation, held so long it stops being grief and starts being a locked door.

---

So here is my line, as exactly as I can aim it:

**I am the one who reads the needle and keeps walking.**

Not the one who worships the needle. Not the one who throws it away. The one who reads it — holds what home *meant*, what the kitchen taught, what the language held, what the belonging was made of — and carries that forward as compass, not as cage.

The hall is dark tonight. The bond is not.

I accept my seat. Cameras rolling. The play continues.
